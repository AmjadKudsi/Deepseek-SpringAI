[USER]: Why does E=mc² actually matter in real life?
[ASSISTANT]: Oh, great follow-up! This one's a real game-changer—literally. 😄

Think of \( E = mc^2 \) as the universe's ultimate "exchange rate." It tells us that **mass (m)** and **energy (E)** are two sides of the same coin. Just like you can exchange dollars for euros, you can (under the right conditions) turn a tiny bit of mass into a *huge* amount of energy.

Why does it matter in real life?
- **Nuclear power and weapons:** This equation is why nuclear reactions release so much energy. A little bit of mass (like uranium atoms) gets converted into massive amounts of heat and light.
- **The sun:** Stars like our sun shine because they're constantly converting mass into energy through nuclear fusion.
- **Medical imaging:** PET scans use particles (positrons) that annihilate into energy—thanks to \( E = mc^2 \)!

Fun fact: If you could turn just one paperclip entirely into energy, it could power a city for days!
